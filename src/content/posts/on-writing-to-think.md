title: "On writing to think"
description: "Writing isn't just recording thoughts — it's how the thinking happens."
pubDate: "2026-06-07"

There's a version of writing where you have a fully formed idea and you write it down. That's not what I do. Or at least, it's not what I want to do here.

The version I find more interesting: you start writing, and the act of writing is what produces the idea. You don't know what you think until you see what you've said.

Paul Graham has written about this. So has Joan Didion — "I write entirely to find out what I'm thinking, what I'm looking at, what I see and what it means." It's a well-worn observation, but it keeps being true.

The practical implication is that writing early is more useful than writing late. If you wait until you're sure what you think, you've already done the thinking and the writing is just transcription. If you write while you're unsure, the writing does the work.

That's what this blog is for.
